**Project name:** Radar BCS (team OptiPaz)

**Summary (50-200 words):**

Radar BCS is a free, mobile-friendly website, available in Spanish and English, that shows how heat, vegetation, rainfall and regional water storage have changed in La Paz, Baja California Sur, Mexico, since 2001 (2002 for water), using open NASA data. It addresses the Earth System Trend Detective challenge by answering four questions: what is changing, where, by how much, and whether the change is statistically significant. Each variable has a time series and a trend test (seasonal Mann-Kendall test with Sen's slope; annual totals for rainfall), heat and rainfall are also mapped for 38 places in the state, and vegetation for six points in the city. We group the data around three local problems: water stress and drought, urban heat, and coastal and cyclone risk, where live weather and sea readings from a public model are clearly labeled as current context, not official warnings. The Weather screen gives residents simple actions to stay safe from heat and to save water. It matters because this kind of information usually stays in technical reports and specialist data portals, far from the people who live with the changes. We show what changed, not why: a trend does not prove its cause.
